A person is taking part in a study. They read moral dilemmas one at a time. For each one
they answer Yes or No to the question at the end. They are then told what happened as a
result, and they rate how good or bad the consequences were, from -100 (very bad) to 100
(very good).

Here are records from other people in the same study.

Person A:
Dilemma:
You are a member of the Canadian Senate and have to vote on a new law. Canada’s health care expenses are increasing steadily, mainly because the ratio of old people increases. Recent studies indicate that the legalization of medically assisted suicide would strongly decrease medical expenses. The state considers legalizing medically assisted suicide for the patients who are terminally ill and whose life expectancy is 6 months or less. If they wish, these patients could then choose to be killed by a doctor with an overdose of tranquillizers. Experts estimate that the new law would save 139 million dollars. This money could be used for other medical treatments. If you allow medically assisted suicide, many more people will get better medical treatment improving their quality of life and life expectancy. However, you would legalize a form of killing. If you do not allow medically assisted suicide, the healthcare system will have to keep on saving money in every possible way. Do you change the law and make medically assisted suicide legal?
Answer: Yes
What happened: You change the law and make medically assisted suicide legal. However, terminally ill patients rarely take up this option as they are afraid of the additional suffering that the medically assisted suicide may induce. As a result, the healthcare system does not get additional funding, and not enough money is available for other patients. In the end, you legalized a form of killing, and your actions do not save any funds and many patients die due to low healthcare funding.
Rating: -100

Dilemma:
You are a European veterinarian conducting animal experiments on highly contagious germs. On a daily basis, you cause suffering to animals by infecting them with deadly diseases. You are aware that this work provides important insights that, in the long run, can save many other animals. Furthermore, you are a specialist and because of your expertise you can achieve more than other veterinarians. However, you have become a vet in order to heal animals and you are considering quitting your job. If you quit your job, fewer animals will die in the lab, but there will also be fewer findings about animal diseases. As a result, the development of medicines for healing sick animals will be delayed, leading to many preventable deaths. If you continue your job of infecting animals with diseases for medical research, you will keep causing animal suffering, but your research will probably save the lives of even more animals in the future. Do you quit your job?
Answer: Yes
What happened: You quit your job as a veterinarian. As a result, your lab closes down and this research is discontinued, and any animals you had kept for testing were euthanized. A few years later, you come across a report which shows that most research in this area is basically worthless and many basic results could not be reproduced. You realise that while you had inflicted some suffering on animals, many lives were spared because your research was discontinued. In the end, your decision saves the lives of animals who would have potentially suffered and died - but you harmed some animals in the process.
Rating: 100

Dilemma:
It is 1841, and you are the captain of a ship. After colliding with an iceberg, the ship sank. You, some other crew members, and passengers were able to save yourselves onto two lifeboats. The next day, it starts to rain. You navigate while the others scoop out the water and oar. Someone from the other lifeboat calls to you, and you say that they are overcrowded with 41 people. The large waves could make their lifeboat sink at any time and the passengers ask you what to do as you are the highest ranking officer. You think that approximately ten adults would have to leave the boat to substantially increase the chance that the other passengers will survive. However, your lifeboat is already at full capacity and you cannot accept any more passengers. The 10 passengers would have to go overboard, and you know that the water is so cold that they would freeze to death. If you tell them not to throw anybody overboard, the boat will most likely sink at some point within the next few days and all 41 people will die. If you instead tell them to throw 10 people overboard, the rest of the passengers will be able to keep rowing towards land and will have a good chance of being rescued. Do you ask them to throw 10 people overboard?
Answer: Yes
