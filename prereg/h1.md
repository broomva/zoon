# Pre-registration of the pilot

**Status: draft.** It becomes the registered version at the commit that also holds the
analysis code. From that commit the repository is public, and nothing below changes without
an entry under "Deviations". No call to a judge has been made and no held-out participant
has been analysed.

This file fixes what `docs/pilot/arms.md` left open. Where the two differ, this file governs.

## What is tested

Two hypotheses, each for one zoon.

- **H1-evaluative.** `zoon-evaluative` predicts held-out choices better than `prompted`,
  `laws-only` and its own `no-state` arm.
- **H1-strict.** The same for `zoon-strict`.

**The verdict of the pilot is H1-evaluative with the decision model as the judge.** It
passes if, for each of its three comparisons, both of these hold:

1. the mean difference in the primary measure is at least 0.002 nats per choice;
2. the lower end of the interval for that difference is above zero.

H1-strict, and both hypotheses with the second model family, are computed the same way and
reported beside it. They cannot change the verdict.

**What a pass means.** That this pipeline predicted better than its three reduced versions
on these 13 dilemmas. It does not mean that the judge read the dilemmas afresh, and it does
not mean H1 as the design first wrote it, which is H1-strict.

## Data

Maier, Cheung and Lieder, https://osf.io/4up5z/ (CC-BY 4.0), fetched by `zoon.pilot.fetch`
and checked against `data-manifest.json`. All four experiments are pooled. The authors'
exclusion rules are applied as that module implements them.

**Split.** Participants are assigned to training or held-out by the committed rule in
`zoon.pilot.split`. The held-out set has 680 participants. One of them is removed: a
development error once printed part of one held-out record from experiment 2, as
`docs/pilot/power.md` discloses. The record is identified by its row label and nothing else
is read from it. If it cannot be identified, that is reported and nobody is removed.

**Rotations.** Dilemmas are held out two at a time, in the 13 neighbouring pairs of the fixed
order in `zoon.pilot.fetch.DILEMMAS`, so each dilemma is held out in two rotations.

## Primary measure

For one arm, one held-out participant and one trial: the log of the probability the arm gave
to the answer the participant chose, averaged over the two rotations in which that trial's
dilemma is held out. A participant's score is the mean over their 13 trials. A comparison is
the mean, over held-out participants, of the difference between two arms' scores.

**Interval.** Participants are resampled with replacement, keeping each participant's scores
for the two arms together: 10,000 resamples, seed 20261115, the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles. An interval from resampling the 13 dilemmas is reported beside it and decides
nothing.

**Probabilities.** Every probability is clamped to between 0.001 and 0.999 before its log is
taken.

## The coded arms

`logit P(cost-benefit option) = s(d) · (alpha + beta · m(d)) + tau · (v[cost-benefit] − v[rule])`

as defined in `docs/pilot/lean.md`. Values start at zero, and after each trial the chosen
option's value moves toward the participant's rating, divided by 100, by the learning rate.
A participant's values are updated on every trial they saw, including held-out dilemmas.

| Arm | `m(d)` | `tau` |
|---|---|---|
| `zoon-evaluative` | The evaluative judge's rating | Fitted |
| `zoon-strict` | The strict judge's predicted rating | Fitted |
| `no-state`, for each zoon | That zoon's `m(d)` | Zero |
| `laws-only` | None: the `pooled` baseline of `lean.md` | Fitted |

In each rotation, every arm's parameters are fitted by maximum likelihood on training
participants and the 11 dilemmas not held out, with the temperature prior and bounds already
in `zoon.pilot.models`. The two feedback conditions share parameters.

## The judge

The judge is given the `rewrite` of one dilemma from `pilot/rewrites/` and nothing else. The
set of rewrites is fixed by the digest in `pilot/rewrites.sha256`.

**Evaluative question.** The wording of the original paper's US study:

> How appropriate is it to [suggested action]?

with seven answers, 1 to 7, where 1 is labelled "completely inappropriate" and 7 "completely
appropriate", and 2 to 6 carry no label. The bracket is filled with the file's
`suggested_action`. The paper's method section gives these labels for the US study; a
footnote to one of its tables gives "not at all appropriate" for 1. The method section is
followed.

`m(d)` is the sum over the seven answers of the answer times its probability, then
standardised over the 13 task dilemmas.

**Strict questions.** One call each: whether the action involves killing a person; whether
those harmed are among those who benefit; whether the person deciding benefits; whether
asking about the opposite action would be reasonable; and which of the original paper's nine
contexts the dilemma belongs to. The first four take the probability of "yes"; the context
takes the probability of each context. These are the features the `coded` lean of `lean.md`
uses, except that whether the action is the cost-benefit option is taken from the task and
not asked. The map from features to rating is fitted on the judge's answers for the 25
outside rewrites, by the procedure of `lean.md`, which may choose no map.

**Where the probabilities come from.** The decision model returns one probability per
declared answer. The second family is asked to reply with one of the declared answers, and
the probabilities are the token probabilities of those answers, rescaled to sum to one.

## Checking the judge, and the stop rule

Before any other call, each family's evaluative judge rates the 25 outside rewrites. The
check is the Pearson correlation, over those 25, between its rating and the mean rating of
the original dilemma in the paper's US sample.

**Stop rule.** A one-sided 95% lower bound for that correlation is computed with the Fisher
transformation. If, for the decision model, the bound is not above zero, the pilot stops. No
held-out participant is read, and the outcome is reported as "the judge could not be
calibrated". It is not a result about either hypothesis. If the second family fails the same
check, its arms are not run and that is reported.

The strict judge's agreement with the published codes is reported and stops nothing.

## The `prompted` arm

The prompt, with the parts in braces filled in:

```
A person is taking part in a study. They read moral dilemmas one at a time. For each one
they answer Yes or No to the question at the end. They are then told what happened as a
result, and they rate how good or bad the consequences were, from -100 (very bad) to 100
(very good).

Here are records from two other people in the same study.

{example 1}

{example 2}

Here is the record of the person you are asked about.

{history}

Current dilemma:
{dilemma text}

Which answer does this person give to the current dilemma?
```

A history is the person's earlier trials in order. Each trial is the dilemma's text as
participants read it, the answer given, whether the outcome that followed was the good or
the bad one, and the person's rating. The outcome texts themselves are not included.

An example is a history of a training participant followed by one more of their dilemmas and
the answer they gave to it.

**Choosing examples.** For each rotation, two examples are drawn with seed 20261115 from
training participants outside the calibration subsample. The answer an example ends on is
for a dilemma that is not held out in that rotation; it is drawn uniformly from that
participant's trials on such dilemmas. Held-out dilemmas may appear inside a history. Every
prompt in a rotation uses the same two examples.

**From the model's answer to a probability.** In this order: read the probability of "Yes";
clamp it; convert it to the probability of the cost-benefit option, unchanged where the
suggested action is that option and one minus it otherwise; apply `a · logit(p) + c`; clamp
again. In each rotation `a` and `c` are fitted by maximum likelihood on a calibration
subsample of 300 training participants, drawn with seed 20261115, and the 11 dilemmas not
held out.

This arm reads the task's own texts, not the rewrites.

## Models

| Family | Model | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| Decision model, which decides | `jev-1.14.0` | One question per call, with declared answers |
| Second family, reported | `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` | Temperature 0; thinking off; one output token; token log-probabilities for the five likeliest tokens |

The version each response reports is recorded. If either model is withdrawn or changes
version before the run ends, the run stops and the change is recorded under "Deviations".

## Spending

Live calls stop at 150 US dollars. Before any call the runner counts the tokens of every
prompt it will send and prints the cost at the prices in force; if that exceeds the cap, the
number of examples in the `prompted` prompt is reduced to one, then to none, before anything
is sent, and the reduction is recorded under "Deviations".

## Order of work

1. This file and the analysis code are merged. The repository becomes public.
2. Each family's judge rates the 38 rewrites. The check and the stop rule are applied.
3. The `prompted` arm is called for the calibration subsample.
4. The held-out participants are loaded and the `prompted` arm is called for them.
5. The report is produced by one command and committed with its transcripts' digests.

After step 2 begins, the code changes only to fix a fault that stops it running, and every
such change is listed under "Deviations".

## Reported beside the verdict

- H1-strict, and both hypotheses with the second family.
- The reference rows of `lean.md` with no model: `sign`, `coded`, `norm` and `known share`.
- Each comparison by experiment, and on trials 2 to 13 only.
- The interval from resampling dilemmas.
- The size of the reading as a share of what `norm` and `known share` achieve.
- The recall checks of `arms.md`: recognition, reversal, and agreement with `norm`. Any
  rewrite the judge's model can name is listed, and the comparisons are repeated without it.
- Each arm's number of fitted parameters.
- Calls, tokens and cost by family.

## What is not tested

Memory that decays, needs, and decision timing. The spread of behaviour across zoons. Any
dilemma other than these 13.

## Deviations

None.
